# More Defect Animations v0.16.0

Version 0.16.0 adds Meteor Strike using the native missile texture, particle curves, impact, smoke and screen shake. The reviewed 1.3x meteor has a white leading tip, Plasma-colored interior and diagonal black debris. Three staggered Plasma trails use the existing trail material and the receiving-slot helper shared with Consuming Shadow. Native damage, channeling, evokes and waits remain unchanged; the requested trail acceleration was cancelled. A separate toggle is available in both settings menus and all three languages.

No image, audio, skeleton or frame-atlas assets are added. The End of Days color batch is linked as source, with no additional mod dependency. Immutable debris and spark shapes are shared; each flight caches its curve once and updates a bounded ribbon mesh. Effects stay in the native combat VFX layer, with rear debris, native core and front debris in order. Measured component CPU is reported in the release record and does not imply a whole-game FPS guarantee.

Version 0.15.9 removes Buffer's subtle dust completely and batches its small impact sparks, keeping the sign recoil and falling fragments. Ice Lance uses smaller flight/wind raster targets, capped fragment resolution, fewer flight blur samples, a fixed trail mesh and cached wind coefficients. Its hit timing, fragment counts, 160% travel speed and layer ordering remain. Fine edges and blur can look softer. Prior wire optimizations and singleplayer preload reuse remain. Scoped performance measurements are not whole-game FPS guarantees.

Historical changes follow.

Version 0.15.8 replaced Charge Battery, Hotfix and Modded cable physics with shared offline shape clips and small attachment corrections. Wire motion and body collision are approximate. Buffer uses smaller textures at the same display size, cached fragment bounds, capped dynamic updates and a warm additive light without a screen copy.

Version 0.15.7 also integrates the approved Modded wire hybrid: shared offline numeric trajectories plus live bone following, per-actor inertia and adaptive length correction. The original seven wires, mesh, textures, masks, 120 Hz timing and card behavior remain. The 0.81 MiB trajectory is loaded once per game process, never simulated during startup or save reload. Floating-point wire shapes can differ from the older full solver, as accepted in the preview. All v0.15.6 optimizations, Ice Lance rendering and singleplayer prewarm reuse are retained. Component timing is not a whole-game FPS guarantee.

Version 0.15.6 applies the approved soft-cable angle simplification to Charge Battery and Hotfix, retaining 120 Hz updates, segment counts, collisions and the original hard bends. Floating-point history can change soft-cable motion slightly, as accepted in the native preview. Flak Cannon caches shared rotations and removes a per-update search allocation; Signal Boost shares repeated rotations across its pipe segments. Geometry checks preserve their vertex results, aim and emitter positions. Existing Ice Lance rendering and singleplayer prewarm reuse remain intact. Modded hybrid precomputation is still a separate preview and is not included. Component CPU savings are not whole-game FPS measurements.

Version 0.15.5 retains completed effect prewarming and the bounded idle Ice Lance pool across singleplayer returns to the menu and saved-run reloads. Active effects, actor bindings and subscriptions still release on exit. Changed settings, interrupted preparation and multiplayer transitions invalidate readiness; an interrupted request can retry after cleanup. The first run after launching the game still warms normally. Retained idle nodes stay hidden with rendering disabled, capped at three instances; disabling Ice Lance or the mod releases them. Animation appearance, previous performance improvements and native save/load behavior are preserved.

Version 0.15.4 reduces continuous work from Signal Boost, Buffer, Modded and Hologram. Resource compatibility checks keep all identity and generation checks inside one native reader call, reducing managed wrappers and allocations. Owned pose snapshots are released immediately, and typed deform reads avoid per-slot finalizable wrappers. The original 120 Hz cable solver is unchanged. Signal ribbons share repeated trigonometry, Buffer retires only particles visible on the previous update, and Hologram selects its closing columns without per-frame sorting allocations. Shared body masks now release when their owning effect exits, even while another effect still uses the body pose. Resolution, blur, geometry, animation clocks, gameplay and the approved Ice Lance optimizations are preserved. Scoped measurements are not a guarantee of whole-battle FPS on every machine.

Version 0.15.3 reduces Ice Lance allocation and drawing overhead while retaining the approved 160% fragment speed, motion blur, particles and resolution. Wind and polygon buffers are reused; strictly convex triangles keep the native index order; two-point antialiased strokes share ordered drawing batches. Immutable materials are shared. Preloading prepares two spare node graphs alongside the existing warm instance; spare render targets shrink to 2x2 while idle, and the pool is bounded to three instances. Repeated burst measurements include creation and cleanup costs. These scoped improvements do not guarantee a fixed whole-battle FPS on every machine.

Version 0.15.2 applies the approved 160% Ice Lance fragment travel speed. Primary pieces, secondary pieces and chips move faster along their own paths; fixed birth headings and backward motion blur use the same speed. Fragment counts, geometry, drag, gravity, split times, lifetimes and opacity are unchanged. The v0.15.1 batching, geometry caches, six shared render targets and pool are retained. Flight before impact, wind, other impact particles, card layers and native card resolution are unchanged.

Version 0.15.1 integrates the approved Ice Lance fragment revision: obvious long tips align within 8 ms of each fracture and then keep their orientation. Short, normalized motion blur follows each fragment's own trajectory. Geometry buffers and local surface information are reused, and the flight crystal's three raster targets are reused after impact for one batched fragment exposure. Compact atlas packing saves filter area; adjacent Ice Lance polygons share ordered drawing batches and reuse their validated triangles. Fragment counts, center trajectories, secondary splitting, lifetimes, other particle effects, card layers and native card resolution are preserved. Blur adds GPU work and texture memory; measured CPU savings are not a guarantee of higher whole-battle FPS on every machine.

Version 0.15.0 adds Gunk Up and Compile Driver from approved HTML revision 12. Gunk Up emits one dirty translucent jet per real hit; Compile Driver uses a short beam, three compact rotating rings and a body-anchored binary wave. Normal (0.150 s), Fast (0.075 s) and Instant presentation observes the native attack callbacks. Damage, draw, Slimed generation, sounds, action speed and original tasks remain native. Cached small ring frames and batched digits require no new runtime viewport, blur pass, skeleton copy or network message. Both cards have independent settings and clean up on cancellation, death, disabled settings or room exit.

Version 0.14.8 precomputes Bulk Up's curved-path coefficients once and interpolates within each authored path segment. A shared table replaces repeated trigonometry and surface transforms; depth sorting moves compact depth/index pairs instead of complete four-vertex packets. All ribbon strips, seven eye flashes, birth anchors, independent timing, body occlusion and native card resolution remain. Render-target resolution and glow passes are unchanged. The table uses approximately 3.3 MiB across both supported rigs and is shared by concurrent casts. The reported single-cast crash remains unconfirmed; these optimizations are not a verified crash fix.

Version 0.14.7 reduces Bulk Up's single-cast update cost and render-target churn. Pose and playback changes are combined before drawing; ribbon cross-sections share calculations, translucent quads share vertices, and only active vertex data plus the previous tail is uploaded. Render targets start at their working size, with the existing resolution ceiling; mesh capacity grows geometrically and birth-event order is cached. Authored ribbon counts, seven eye flashes, occlusion, independent cast timing and native card resolution are retained. The reported single-cast crash has not yet been reproduced or traced to a confirmed cause.

Version 0.14.6 packs Ice Lance wind into three shared raster passes and reuses wind geometry calculations. Hologram uses adaptive 30/60 Hz source sampling and refreshes blur only for a new pose; native skeleton animation and projection opening/closing remain independent. Modded shares cable vertices and caches identical guide poses while retaining the full 120 Hz cable solver and live skin binding.

Version 0.14.5 reduces raster density for Ice Lance wind, Hologram, Modded masks and Supercritical flow. Bulk Up grows bounded render capacity in blocks instead of resizing every pose; 3D and shadow passes render only when updated. Geometry, pose timing, world placement, blur widths and native card resolution remain independent of raster density. Sharp Ice Lance fragments from v0.14.4 are retained.

Version 0.14.4 removes Ice Lance fragment motion blur and impact-particle trails. Sharp fracture faces draw directly in batches, eliminating the fragment render atlas and per-piece exposure shaders. Fragment counts, trajectories, secondary splitting, lifetimes and native card resolution remain intact. Flight-crystal and wind rendering retain their existing appearance.

Version 0.14.3 restores Flak Cannon's per-hit angular scatter: the cannon and beam pivot randomly within +/-7 degrees, while the impact effect stays at the monster's native hit position. Changes blend over 45 ms, hold between hits and return to centre during the tail. Target selection, damage, game RNG, recoil and card timing remain unchanged.

### Godot-derived antialiased stroke tessellation

The Ice Lance two-point stroke batching helper adapts the Godot 4.5 polyline tessellation algorithm from `servers/rendering/renderer_canvas_cull.cpp` (https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/4.5/servers/rendering/renderer_canvas_cull.cpp).
